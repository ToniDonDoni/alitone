# Music specification

## Original brief

Dark, bass-heavy industrial military march. 108 BPM, strict 2/4 marching cadence. Relentless, disciplined, mechanical military procession.

DOMINANT MASSIVE LOW-PITCHED DRUMS. Deep military bass drums, thunderous low toms, tightly synchronized marching snare drums, powerful rhythmic impacts. Heavy LEFT-RIGHT marching pulse from the first second to the end. Percussion is the loudest element in the mix.

Extremely low bass trombones, tubas and contrabass brass playing short, dark, repetitive marching motifs. Deep distorted synthesizer bass, sub-bass pulses, ominous industrial rumbling, metallic rhythmic textures.

Low-register instrumentation throughout. Minimal, sinister, repetitive melody. Dark minor-key harmonies, unsettling dissonance, relentless psychological tension.

A terrifying military procession carrying a dead dictator through an ancient cemetery toward a forbidden resurrection ritual.

Oppressive, threatening, cold, monumental, hypnotic. Heavy cinematic low-frequency sound design, but unmistakably a military march, never an orchestral symphony.

No high-pitched brass, no trumpets, no violins, no strings, no organ, no bright fanfares, no soaring melodies, no choir, no vocals, no heroic or celebratory atmosphere.

Instrumental. DRUMS FIRST, SUB-BASS SECOND, LOW BRASS THIRD.

## Delivery and reference matching

- Deliver one self-contained local HTML page with inline JavaScript. Generate the music procedurally in Web Audio and keep it playing indefinitely after the play button is pressed. Use the MP3 as a comparison reference.
- Keep strict 2/4. The initial brief called for 108 BPM. The later listening direction favored the slower pace, so the page defaults to 80 BPM, near the measured opening of the reference. The tempo slider also supports 108 BPM.
- The user recalled music starting at about 2:33 in a longer recording. The supplied AIFC is about 38.83 s long, with the first audible material at about 2.99 s. The reference MP3 was cut at 2.98 s of that file. The mapping to the longer recording is unknown.
- Compare the same opening phrases, starting with the drums in isolation. Match the short beater attack, the rise in low-frequency resonance after the strike, and the snare-wire buildup into the next step. Add accented sub-bass and short low brass responses after the cadence is in place.
- Maintain separate synthesis voices for the large drum, toms, snare, wire roll, metal, sub-bass, brass, and rumble. Tune timing, envelope, frequency movement, and relative dynamics per voice.
